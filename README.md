# Canada's Productivity Rebound: A Turning Point or a Better Quarter?

**Williams Economic Consulting · Independent Economic Research & Commentary**  
**Researcher:** Marcus Williams  
**Published:** October 2026

## Overview

Canada's business-sector labour productivity increased in the second quarter of 2026. This research examines what the rebound can—and cannot—tell us about Canada's longer-term productivity performance.

The central question is whether the improvement reflects sustained efficiency gains, stronger utilization of existing capacity, changes in industry composition, or a combination of these factors.

## Research questions

- How does the latest productivity increase compare with Canada's longer-run performance?
- Which industries are driving the change in aggregate productivity?
- What role do capital investment and capital deepening play?
- How does Canada's productivity growth compare with that of the United States?
- What evidence would distinguish a temporary rebound from a structural improvement?

## Sources

The research draws on public data and analysis from Statistics Canada, the Bank of Canada, the OECD and the US Bureau of Labor Statistics.

## Research approach

The project distinguishes labour productivity from capital deepening and multifactor productivity. It documents data definitions, sources, calculations and limitations so that findings can be evaluated and reproduced.

## Status

This project is being developed as independent economic commentary and quantitative research. Conclusions will be updated as historical comparisons and additional empirical tests are completed.
